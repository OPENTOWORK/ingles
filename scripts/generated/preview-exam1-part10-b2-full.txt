QUESTION 1
================================================================================

Situation:
  A visitor is asking about a painting in a local art gallery.

Audio script (~78 words | est. ~29 s):
  I'm afraid the Constable landscape isn't in the main gallery today. We've lent
  the original to a partner museum for their spring exhibition, which is why
  you'll see a high-quality reproduction on the wall. It should return in June
  when the show closes. If nineteenth-century landscapes interest you, the Turner
  watercolours in room four are part of our permanent collection — they don't
  travel, and staff say they're among the most popular works we own.

Question:
  What does the speaker recommend the visitor do next?

  A) Book a guided tour before leaving the building
  B) Come back when the borrowed painting has returned
  C) Look at other works displayed in a nearby room

Correct answer: C

Why the answer works:
  The speaker points the visitor toward the Turner watercolours in room four as
  something worth seeing now. A is never offered; B is a long-term possibility,
  not the immediate recommendation. Correct option paraphrases “room four” rather
  than repeating “Turner watercolours”.

QUESTION 2
================================================================================

Situation:
  An announcement is being made at an airport departure gate.

Audio script (~82 words | est. ~31 s):
  Attention passengers travelling to Lisbon on flight TP fourteen twenty-three.
  Due to a last-minute aircraft change, boarding will now take place at gate
  twenty-two, not gate nine as shown on your boarding pass. Please make your way
  to the new gate without delay. Boarding will begin in approximately ten minutes.
  All checked baggage has already been loaded onto the replacement aircraft.
  Passengers requiring special assistance should speak to a member of staff at
  gate twenty-two. We apologise for the late notice and any inconvenience this
  may cause.

Question:
  What are passengers told to do?

  A) Collect their luggage before going to the gate
  B) Go to a different gate from the one on their boarding pass
  C) Wait where they are until the delay is officially over

Correct answer: B

Why the answer works:
  Boarding moves from gate nine to gate twenty-two. A misreads baggage handling
  (bags are already loaded); C contradicts “make your way … without delay”.

Situation:
  A student is telephoning a university registry office about an official transcript.

Audio script (~76 words | est. ~28 s):
  Thanks for calling Registry. An official transcript isn't something we can print
  while you wait — we need a signed application form and a copy of your student ID.
  Processing normally takes five working days. There is an express service if you're
  willing to pay an additional fee, but the standard request still has to go
  through the same approval process. You can download the form from the student
  portal or collect one from the desk in the lobby.

Question:
  What must the student do to obtain a transcript?

  A) Submit a completed application together with identification
  B) Ask their personal tutor to send it directly to an employer
  C) Wait until after graduation before the request can be processed

Correct answer: A

Why the answer works:
  Form plus ID are explicitly required. B and C reflect common assumptions but are
  not stated. “Identification” paraphrases “student ID”.
