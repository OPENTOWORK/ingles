QUESTION 1
================================================================================

Situation:
  You hear two friends discussing a weekend walking trip.

Audio script (~88 words | est. ~36 s):
  A: I'm gasping for a weekend away — work's been piling up and I can't switch off.
  B: Same, though if the mist rolls in on Sunday, we may have to call the ridge walk off.
  A: The forecast looks settled enough. What bothers me is we still haven't sorted out
     who's bringing the stove.
  B: Liam was going to look into hiring one from the outdoor shop on Mill Lane.
  A: He's had to pull out at short notice — his dad's been taken into hospital — so
     unless one of us steps in, we'll be stuck with cold food on the summit.

Question:
  What has caused a difficulty for the women's plans?

  A) Unexpectedly bad weather in the forecast
  B) A problem with transport to the starting point
  C) The person who was going to provide equipment can no longer do so

Correct answer: C

Why the answer works:
  Liam was arranging the stove but has pulled out. A mentions possible mist but says
  the forecast looks settled; B is never mentioned. "Equipment" paraphrases "stove".

Language focus: call off, sort out, look into, pull out, at short notice, steps in

  Remain inside the terminal building and do not proceed to lane four, where queuing

Question:
  What are passengers told to do?

  A) Stay in the terminal until check-in opens
  B) Join the queue at lane four as quickly as possible
  C) Collect their vehicles from the car park before midday

Correct answer: A

Why the answer works:
  They must remain inside until check-in opens. B contradicts "do not proceed to
  lane four"; C invents car-park collection.

Language focus: proceed to, queuing, due to, ensure

QUESTION 3
================================================================================

Situation:
  You hear a woman telephoning a dental surgery.

Audio script (~88 words | est. ~35 s):
  A: Good morning, Riverside Dental — how can I help?
  B: Hi, it's Rachel Masters. I need to make an appointment — I've been putting off
     a check-up for ages, and I've got a sharp pain when I bite down.
  A: The earliest routine slot is three weeks away. I can put you down for emergency
     triage tomorrow at half past four, but bring your NHS number.
  B: Tomorrow works — I'd rather get it sorted properly than have something temporary
     done.
  A: The dentist will decide when she sees you. Could you arrive ten minutes early
     to fill in a form?

Question:
  Why is the woman calling?

  A) To arrange to be seen about a dental problem she has been ignoring
  B) To cancel an appointment she can no longer attend
  C) To complain about a temporary repair that has come loose

Correct answer: A

Why the answer works:
  She has put off a check-up and now has pain. B and C are not the purpose of the call;
  "temporary" refers to treatment she wants to avoid, not a failed repair.

Language focus: putting off, put you down for, get it sorted, bite down

QUESTION 4

Situation:
  You hear two flatmates talking about their washing machine.

Audio script (~103 words | est. ~39 s):
  A: Have you seen the washing machine? It cut out mid-cycle again last night.
  B: I noticed — it left my gym kit sitting in soapy water for hours.
  A: I had a look at the filter, but I can't get the panel off without the right tool.
     I rang the agency, and they said minor breakdowns are down to us to sort out.
  B: Great. I suppose we could try that repair shop on Garston Road — they fixed my
     mate's dryer last year, and he said they didn't rip him off.
  A: Worth a shot. Just don't go ahead and order a new one online — we can't afford
     to shell out for that right now.

Question:
  What do the men decide to do about the washing machine?

  A) Report the fault and wait for the agency to send someone official
  B) Look online for a replacement appliance they can install themselves
  C) Try getting it repaired locally rather than buying a new one

Correct answer: C

Why the answer works:
  They choose the Garston Road repair shop. A reflects what the agency said, not their
  decision; A explicitly rules out ordering online (B).

Language focus: cut out, down to us, sort out, rip off, shell out, go ahead

Situation:
  You hear a recorded message from a gym.

Audio script (~95 words | est. ~37 s):
  Hi, it's Grace from FitWorks. Your membership comes up for renewal next Friday.
  We've brought in a new off-peak tariff that works out cheaper if you mainly train

  staying in the area, pop in and we can talk you through the options — otherwise,
  give us a ring so we don't keep charging you by mistake. Either way, could you get
  back to us before the end of the week?

Question:
  What is the purpose of the message?

  A) To confirm that the woman's membership has already been cancelled
  B) To find out whether the woman still intends to remain a member
  C) To tell her about a lower-cost membership she may want to consider

Correct answer: B

Why the answer works:
  Grace needs to know if the member is relocating or staying (renewal/charging). A is
  false; C mentions the tariff but the main purpose is clarifying membership status.
  Distractor C echoes "off-peak tariff" without being the primary aim.

Language focus: comes up for renewal, works out cheaper, run out, talk you through

Situation:
  You hear a conversation in a bookshop.

Audio script (~102 words | est. ~38 s):
  A: Can I help at all?
  B: I'm trying to track down a novel for my mum — she read a review in the paper,
     but I've drawn a blank online.
  A: Do you recall whether it was historical fiction? We've got a table of new releases
     by the window.
  B: It's set in Belfast during the seventies, I think — the title had the word
     "bridge" in it, but I might be mixing it up with something else.
  A: That rings a bell — we sold out last week, but I could order a copy in. It
     usually takes four or five days to come in.
  B: Perfect — she'll be chuffed if it arrives before her birthday.

Question:
  What does the man offer to do?

  A) Order a book that is not currently available in the shop
  B) Check whether another branch has the book in stock today
  C) Recommend a similar novel from the display by the window

Correct answer: A

Why the answer works:
  The book sold out; he offers to order a copy. B (another branch) is never offered;
  C (window display) is a misread of the historical-fiction table mention.

Language focus: track down, drawn a blank, rings a bell, sold out, order in

QUESTION 8
================================================================================

Situation:
  You hear a tenant speaking to a letting agent on the phone.

Audio script (~108 words | est. ~41 s):
  A: Hi, it's Priya from the agency. I'm following up on your email about the noise
     from next door.
  B: Thanks for ringing back. It's mostly bass through the wall late at night — it's
     hard to get to sleep.
  A: I've spoken to your neighbour. He's willing to turn the volume down after eleven,
     but he won't rip the speakers out altogether.
  B: That's a start, I suppose, though it doesn't really get to the bottom of it.
  A: Legally we can't evict someone over a stereo unless it's formally logged over
     several weeks. I'd suggest keeping a written record, and we'll review it at the
     month-end inspection.
  B: OK — I'll note down the dates and times.

Question:
  What does the agent advise the tenant to do?

  A) Withhold part of the rent until the neighbour removes the equipment
  B) Keep a written record of when the disturbance happens
  C) Arrange a meeting with the neighbour without involving the agency

Correct answer: B

Why the answer works:
  Priya explicitly suggests keeping a written record. A (withhold rent) and C (private
  meeting) are not advised.

Language focus: following up, ringing back, get to the bottom of, formally logged
