TITLE
================================================================================

Bringing the Hall Back to Life

================================================================================
INSTRUCTIONS
================================================================================

You will hear an interview with a woman called Nina who helped to restore an old
market hall in her town. For questions 24–30, choose the best answer (A, B or C).

================================================================================
FULL INTERVIEW SCRIPT (unchanged — ~548 words | est. 3:30–4:00)
================================================================================

A: Today I'm speaking to Nina Ortiz, who has spent the last three years working on
a project to reopen the town's old market hall. Nina, what condition was the
building in when you first saw it?

B: Honestly, most people walked past without looking up. The roof leaked, the
floorboards were unsafe in places, and pigeons had got in through broken windows.
The council had boarded up the main doors years earlier because they said repairs
would cost too much.

A: How did the restoration project actually begin?

B: It didn't start with a big grant or a famous architect. A handful of residents
formed a small trust after a public meeting about empty buildings in the high
street. We asked the council for a temporary lease, not ownership, and they agreed
because nothing else was happening with the site.

A: Where did the money come from in the early stages?

B: People assume we waited for government funding, but the first tools, paint and
safety equipment were bought with donations from local businesses and a
crowdfunding page. A national heritage body gave us a substantial sum later, once
we could show detailed plans and support from architects.

A: What was the biggest practical difficulty during the work?

B: Finding skilled volunteers wasn't the problem — we had electricians and
carpenters who lived nearby. The headache was the hidden damage behind the walls.
Every time we opened a section to check it, we discovered timber that needed
replacing, which delayed the timetable and meant reordering materials.

A: The hall hosts events now. Who uses the space?

B: We imagined it would mainly be evening classes for adults, but families with
young children use it most during the weekend. There's a small café run by a social
enterprise, and a charity rents an office at the back, but the busy hours are
definitely Saturday and Sunday mornings.

A: Has the project changed how people see the town centre?

B: Shopkeepers told us footfall dropped when the hall closed. Now some say
customers come specifically because there's a reason to stay longer — not just to
collect a parcel and leave. I wouldn't claim we've saved every business, but the
street feels less forgotten.

A: What advice would Nina give to another town thinking of a similar project?

B: Don't wait until you have a perfect plan on paper. Start with one achievable
room, prove the building can be warm and dry, and let the community see progress.
If you spend two years only producing reports, volunteers lose heart and the
council moves on to other priorities.

A: Nina, thank you for talking to us.

B: My pleasure.

QUESTIONS 24–30 — OPTIONS A / B / C
================================================================================

24. What does Nina say about the building when she first saw it?

    A) The interior had recently been repaired by the council
    B) It had serious structural and weather-related problems
    C) It was still being used for regular farmers' markets

25. What arrangement did the project group obtain from the council?

    A) Immediate transfer of full ownership to the trust
    B) A contract for a private company to redevelop the site
    C) A temporary lease allowing work to begin on the building

26. What does Nina say about how the earliest costs were covered?

    A) They were mainly paid for by local donations before larger grants arrived
    B) The council paid for all equipment and materials from the start
    C) A heritage organisation financed the entire project in one payment

27. What caused the most delay during the restoration work?

    A) Difficulty recruiting electricians and carpenters from outside the town
    B) Damage discovered inside walls that had not been visible earlier
    C) Repeated changes to the colour scheme chosen for the main hall

28. Who uses the hall most at present, according to Nina?

    A) Adults attending weekday evening courses
    B) Office workers using the space at lunchtimes on weekdays
    C) Families with young children at weekends

29. What change has Nina noticed in the town centre since the hall reopened?

    A) Some people now stay in the area longer instead of leaving straight away
    B) Every independent shop has reported a large rise in profits
    C) Most visitors now come only to use the hall café and then go home

30. What does Nina suggest towns in a similar situation should do first?

    A) Publish a complete set of architectural reports before any work starts
    B) Open one practical section of the building so progress is visible
    C) Postpone volunteer work until a national expert agrees to lead the team

WHY EACH ANSWER WORKS
================================================================================

24 → B
Nina describes a leaking roof, unsafe floorboards, broken windows and boarded-up
doors — clear structural and weather damage. The council had NOT recently repaired
it (A). The building was disused, not hosting markets (C).

25 → C
She states explicitly they asked for a temporary lease, not ownership. They did not
receive full ownership (A). The project began with a residents' trust, not a
private redeveloper contracted by the council (B).

26 → A
First tools and equipment came from local business donations and crowdfunding; the
national heritage sum came later with detailed plans. The council did not fund
everything from the start (B). Heritage money came later and did not cover the
entire project in one go (C).

27 → B
Nina says skilled volunteers were available locally; delays came from hidden timber
damage found when opening walls. Recruiting tradespeople was NOT the problem (A).
Colour scheme disputes are never mentioned (C).

28 → C
She says families with young children use the hall most at weekends (busy Saturday
and Sunday mornings). Evening adult classes were what they expected, not what
happened (A). A charity rents an office, but lunchtimes on weekdays are not
described as the busiest use (B).

29 → A
Shopkeepers say customers stay longer — a reason to remain, not just collect a
parcel and leave. Nina explicitly would NOT claim every shop has been saved or
profited (B). She does not say visitors only use the café and leave (C); the point
is they stay in the area more generally.

30 → B
Her advice is to start with one achievable room and show the building can be warm
and dry. She warns against spending years on reports only — the opposite of (A).
She never says to wait for a national expert before volunteer work (C).
