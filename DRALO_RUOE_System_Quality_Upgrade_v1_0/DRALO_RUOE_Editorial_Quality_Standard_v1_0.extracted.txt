DRALO

RUOE EDITORIAL QUALITY STANDARD

Global language, authenticity and title rules for B2 RUOE generation

Version 1.0 · 14 August 2026



1. Purpose

This standard is a global quality layer for Parts 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7. It does not replace Topic Bank, Style Cards, Content Briefs or Part mechanics. It governs whether generated language is publishable-quality DRALO English after the structural requirements have been met.

Core principle

A sentence must not merely be grammatical. It must be natural, idiomatic, genre-appropriate British English at B2 level. Exercise mechanics must adapt to natural language, not the other way round.



2. Global editorial rules

ID

Rule

Operational meaning

EQS-01

Naturalness before item convenience

If an item, stem, option or gap forces awkward wording, redesign the item rather than preserve the awkward sentence.

EQS-02

Idiomatic collocation

Prefer common, defensible lexical combinations. Reject technically possible but noticeably unnatural collocations.

EQS-03

Concrete before abstract

Prefer concrete verbs, actions and situations over unnecessary nominalisation, corporate language or vague abstraction.

EQS-04

Genre authenticity

The text should sound like a plausible magazine, feature, report, narrative or profile, not like instructions translated into prose.

EQS-05

No filler

Every paragraph must add a new event, fact, contrast, perspective, example, consequence or inference. Repetition to reach word count is not acceptable.

EQS-06

Controlled repetition

Key lexical repetition may support cohesion, but repeated conclusions, repeated evaluative language or repeated abstract nouns must be flagged.

EQS-07

B2 control

Do not increase complexity simply to sound sophisticated. Natural B2 is preferable to forced C1/C2 phrasing.

EQS-08

British English

Prefer natural British usage where alternatives exist, without forcing artificial Britishisms.

EQS-09

Item invisibility

If the exercise mechanics were removed, the remaining text should still read like natural prose.

EQS-10

Adversarial review

A validator must actively try to disprove answer uniqueness and naturalness; generator self-claims are not sufficient evidence.



3. Title Quality Standard

Titles are editorial elements, not labels copied from the Content Brief. The generator should create multiple candidates internally and select one after checking naturalness, originality and recent title patterns.

• TQ-01 · Do not paraphrase the brief literally.

• TQ-02 · Do not reveal the complete lesson, conclusion or answer of the text.

• TQ-03 · Avoid repeated templates such as “The X that…”, “What happens when…”, “When X…” across a batch.

• TQ-04 · Prefer a concrete image, intriguing observation, understated contrast, concise statement, personal realisation or genuinely useful question.

• TQ-05 · Title wording must be natural for the assigned Style Card and text genre.

• TQ-06 · Generate at least 3 candidate titles internally; score them for naturalness, distinctiveness, genre fit and overlap with recent titles.

• TQ-07 · Usage History should store final title plus a coarse title-pattern family so repetitive patterns can be detected longitudinally.

4. Editorial Quality Validator

Run this as a separate pass after the Part-specific structural validator. It should not be the same generation call that produced the exercise.

Dimension

Validator behaviour

Naturalness

Flag awkward collocations, translated-sounding phrasing, over-abstract language and sentences that a competent editor would normally rewrite.

Redundancy

Detect repeated ideas, repeated conclusions, filler paragraphs and excessive recurrence of the same lexical family.

Genre fit

Check that voice, register and paragraph movement match the assigned Style Card.

Title quality

Check title-pattern repetition, literal brief paraphrase, genericness and over-explanation.

Item interference

Ask whether the text has been distorted to accommodate a gap, stem, option or question.

Adversarial answer test

Attempt to justify every distractor or alternative answer. More than one defensible answer is a fail.

Reference integrity

Verify paragraph references, quotations, pronouns and cited locations against the actual passage.

6. Scope boundaries

• Topic Bank is not changed by these findings: the pilot issues concern language realisation and item construction, not thematic selection.

• Distribution Rules remain responsible for variety across exams; title-pattern history can later be added as a longitudinal soft control.

• Style Cards define genre-specific voice; this standard defines quality that applies to every Style Card.

• Part prompts define exercise mechanics; validators enforce rules so they cannot be forgotten in future generations.
