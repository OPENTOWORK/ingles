2. Cross-Part prompt changes

• Generate natural prose first; item construction must not degrade the text.

• Before finalising, rewrite any sentence whose main justification is ‘it makes the item work’.

• Run an internal title-candidate stage after the text is stable.

• Do not rely on generator self-assessment for answer uniqueness; pass output to independent validators.

• Repair locally: sentence/item/question before whole exercise; whole exercise only when the structure is fundamentally unsalvageable.

3. Part-specific changes

Part 1 · Multiple-choice cloze

• Build each item around a genuine lexical/collocational distinction in a natural sentence.

• Adversarially test all four options. If two options are defensible in context, the item fails.

• Do not rebalance A–D by changing the correct answer. Only reorder intact option sets when semantically safe.

• Distractors must fail for an explainable lexical, collocational, semantic or grammatical reason.

Part 2 · Open cloze

• Prefer gaps that test meaningful function words/grammar rather than trivially obligatory continuations when the batch becomes too easy.

• Naturalness of surrounding prose overrides desire to force a particular preposition/conjunction.

• Check that each gap has one defensible one-word answer in context.

Part 3 · Word formation

• Require genuine derivational variety across the eight scored items: category changes, suffixes, prefixes and negative formation where natural.

• Root identical to answer does not count as a valid transformation unless explicitly justified by the official mechanic; default action is replace the item.

• If a planned stem produces an unnatural sentence, change the stem/item rather than force the sentence.

• Validator must flag a batch with no prefix/negative formation or excessive same-type transformations.

• Natural sentence first, transformation second.

Part 5 · Multiple-choice reading

• Construct distractors mainly from real passage information, but distort relation, cause, scope, attitude, speaker, timing or implication.

• Reject distractors that can be discarded because the text never mentions the idea at all, unless strategically justified.

• Verify every textual reference (‘last paragraph’, quoted phrase, pronoun) against the final passage.

• Reduce literal word matching between question/option and evidence.

Part 7 · Multiple matching

• Questions should paraphrase evidence rather than reuse the decisive wording from a profile.

• Each question must require discrimination of meaning, attitude, motivation, timing, cause or outcome.

• Run a lexical-overlap check; high overlap with exactly one profile is a warning and may require rewriting.

4. Part 6 Generation Architecture v2

Structural correction

Part 6 must be generated as a true gapped-text task. The six correct A–G options are sentences removed from the article; they must not remain duplicated in the passage.



Step

Required behaviour

P6-V2-01

Generate a complete, coherent article from the approved Content Brief and Style Card, with no gaps yet.

P6-V2-02

Identify six genuine cohesion opportunities at paragraph/inter-paragraph level.

P6-V2-03

For each candidate, record: discourse function, backward anchor, forward anchor, reference chain, logical relation and why it should fit only one position.

P6-V2-04

Write/select one complete removable sentence for each opportunity. A Cambridge-like option is normally one sentence, but it may be developed enough to occupy several visual lines.

P6-V2-05

Physically remove the six sentences from the article and replace them with gaps 37–42.

P6-V2-06

Create one plausible unused sentence that shares topic/lexis but fails at least one essential cohesion relation in every gap.

P6-V2-07

Shuffle the seven options A–G only after extraction.

P6-V2-08

Run structural, uniqueness and editorial validators before output.



5. Part 6 option design standard

• Each A–G option is normally one complete sentence, not 3–4 separate sentences.

• Options may be substantially longer than the pilot outputs; Cambridge reference samples include both concise and developed single-sentence options.

• Use a soft editorial target rather than a hard word count: roughly 8–30 words is acceptable when cohesion requires it; justified exceptions are allowed.

• An option should carry meaningful discourse: reference, contrast, consequence, reformulation, exemplification, continuation, cause, concession or temporal development.

• Do not shorten an option merely to hit a target if that removes the cohesion signal.

• Across the seven options, vary cohesion mechanisms; do not make every answer solvable by one pronoun or one repeated noun.

• The correct option must make sense with both the preceding and following text, not merely with one side.

6. Part 6 HARD validators

ID

Hard check

P6-H01

Exactly 6 gaps numbered 37–42.

P6-H02

Exactly 7 options A–G and exactly one unused option.

P6-H03

No option A–G may remain verbatim in the gapped passage.

P6-H04

Each correct option fits one and only one gap.

P6-H05

No gap has two fully defensible answers.

P6-H06

Unused option fits no gap fully.

P6-H07

Correct option must have a documented cohesion relation to both local sides of the gap.

P6-H08

Final passage plus inserted correct options reconstructs coherent prose without duplication.



7. Quality/adversarial validators

• Try every wrong option in every gap and explain why it fails. If the explanation is weak or subjective, flag for regeneration.

• Check that options are not all trivially short or generic.

• Check lexical overlap: repeated nouns may support cohesion but must not make answers obvious by keyword matching alone.

• Check paragraph progression after reinsertion of all six answers.

• Check title and prose using Editorial Quality Validator after P6 mechanics pass.

8. Acceptance gate before scaling

Do not treat these rules as validated merely because they have been implemented. Generate a second controlled pilot and require human review. Only promote to production-scale generation after the new validators catch seeded bad cases and teachers confirm a material reduction in naturalness/item-quality problems.
