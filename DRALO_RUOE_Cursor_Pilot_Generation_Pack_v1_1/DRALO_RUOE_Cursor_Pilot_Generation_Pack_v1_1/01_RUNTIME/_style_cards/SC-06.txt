SC-06  ·  STYLE CARD

Multiple Profiles & Testimonies

STATUSReady for pilot validation

LANGUAGEBritish English

LEVELCEFR B2

What content it suits

Part 7 content built from four people/profiles who share a common theme but differ in experience, attitude, motivation, difficulty, response or outcome.

Compatible Parts

Part 7.

Typical opening

• Use a concise shared frame or title that makes the comparison domain clear.

• Each profile should enter quickly through a distinctive personal fact, attitude, episode or problem rather than repeating the shared topic.

• Profiles do not need identical openings; controlled variation helps voice differentiation.

Content progression

• Give each profile a self-contained mini-arc with 2–4 comparable dimensions.

• Distribute overlapping concepts across profiles so questions require discrimination rather than keyword spotting.

• Make some profiles share surface similarities while differing in cause, attitude, timing, motivation or consequence.

• Ensure every profile contains multiple examinable ideas and that no single profile becomes obviously richer than the others.

• Across the set, vary the order in which comparable dimensions appear.

What gives it real substance

• Concrete episodes and decisions unique to each person.

• Distinctive attitude and evaluative language.

• Comparable dimensions: expectations, obstacles, reactions, benefits, regrets, motivation, change, advice, relationships or outcomes.

• Paraphrasable evidence: the question wording should not merely repeat the profile’s key words.

Tone / voice

Natural, differentiated voices. First person works especially well, but third-person mini-profiles are also valid. Each person should sound plausible without exaggerated dialect or caricature.

Typical ending

• Each profile should end on its own meaningful state: outcome, current view, consequence, reflection or next step.

• Avoid giving all four profiles the same rhetorical ending or identical 'now I...' structure.

Avoid

• Four versions of the same story with names changed.

• One profile containing nearly all the strong examinable material.

• Obvious lexical matching between question and answer.

• Artificially forcing every profile through the same paragraph template.

• Voices differentiated only by superficial hobbies or demographic labels.

• Contradictory or insufficient evidence for the matching questions.

References used

7.1 — Four people describe how they turned their hobbies into new careers. Excellent comparison set: common transformation theme, but different triggers, obstacles, confidence levels, commercial decisions and skill development.

7.6 — What was university like for you?. Strong differentiated voices with overlapping dimensions—money, study, career, identity and mistakes—distributed in different combinations.

7.5 — Four rising stars. Demonstrates third-person/quoted mini-profiles with distinct developmental paths, setbacks, expectations and chance opportunities.

ENGINE NOTE  Part 7 requires this family because the content itself must be architected for cross-profile matching. The Content Brief should explicitly plan comparable dimensions and differentiation before the structural prompt writes the ten questions.



8. Pilot validation checklist

These cards are now written and referenced. The remaining step is practical validation with new DRALO content—not further theoretical expansion.

• Generate at least two new Content Briefs from each Style Card using different Topic Bank entries.

• Check whether the generated briefs are recognisably different across Style Cards even when topics are similar.

• Check whether each card creates enough substance without forcing invented facts.

• Check whether Parts 1–3 can compress the style naturally without sounding formulaic.

• Check whether Parts 5–6 retain strong paragraph progression and support the construct of the Part.

• For SC-06, verify that four profiles are differentiated yet comparable and that matching questions can be paraphrased without keyword dependence.

• Record Keep / Change / Add / Remove / Do not feedback and revise only where the pilot exposes a repeatable problem.
