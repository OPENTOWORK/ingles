SC-03  ·  STYLE CARD

Personal Reflective Experience

STATUSReady for pilot validation

LANGUAGEBritish English

LEVELCEFR B2

What content it suits

First-person experiences in which the value comes from a change in understanding, a decision, an unexpected difficulty, a discovered preference or a personal reinterpretation of something familiar.

Compatible Parts

Parts 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6.

Typical opening

• Open from a personal situation, habit, expectation or decision that gives the reader a concrete starting point.

• Signal the narrator’s attitude early, but leave room for that attitude to change.

• Small personal details are preferable to a generic autobiographical introduction.

Content progression

• Set up the narrator’s initial expectation or normal behaviour.

• Introduce an experience, obstacle, discovery or contradiction.

• Show the narrator reacting, adapting or reassessing.

• Use concrete episodes rather than summarising the whole experience abstractly.

• Finish with what changed in the narrator’s view, behaviour or sense of value.

What gives it real substance

• Specific moments, choices, frustrations, surprises and sensory or situational detail.

• A clear before/after contrast in perspective.

• Self-awareness: the narrator can admit uncertainty, embarrassment, mistaken assumptions or mixed feelings.

• Reflection should grow out of events; it should not be pasted on as a life lesson.

Tone / voice

Natural first-person magazine voice. Conversational but controlled; reflective without sounding therapeutic. Light humour, self-deprecation and small asides are welcome when natural.

Typical ending

• Close on a changed perspective, earned satisfaction, lingering question or concrete intention.

• The ending should feel specific to the experience, not like a universal motivational slogan.

Avoid

• Generic 'this taught me to never give up' morals.

• Chronological diary-style listing with no change in perspective.

• Overdramatic emotional language.

• Perfect narrators who never make mistakes or reconsider anything.

• Reflection that is not supported by concrete events.

References used

5.1 — My book collection. Excellent reflective arc: attachment → embarrassment/problem → attempted solution → rediscovery → more nuanced understanding of self and others.

5.5 — My first summer job. Expectation versus reality, several concrete episodes, adaptation and a final moment where the narrator discovers a genuine strength.

6.6 — The wild attraction of competitive fell running. Personal entry point, remembered first encounter, development into commitment and an explicit but experience-based explanation of why the activity matters.

ENGINE NOTE  For Parts 1–3, keep the narrator and change-of-perspective mechanism but reduce the number of episodes. For Part 6, the progression must also provide strong discourse links between paragraphs for the structural prompt to exploit.
