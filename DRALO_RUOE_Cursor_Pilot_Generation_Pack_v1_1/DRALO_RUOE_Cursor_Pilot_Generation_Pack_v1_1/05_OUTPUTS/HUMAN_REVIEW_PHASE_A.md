### Vista alumno

**Part title:** Small Changes That Made Work Feel Better

**Instructions**

You are going to read four profiles of people who describe a change in their workplace that improved their daily experience. For questions 43–52, choose from the profiles A–D. The profiles may be chosen more than once.

Why can different people value very different changes in the way their work is organised?

**Common context / title:** Four People Describe Workplace Changes

#### A. A

As a designer and analyst, my job requires long periods of deep concentration, but constant interruptions made it hard to complete complex tasks. Recently, our team introduced no-meeting focus blocks, allowing us to work undisturbed for set periods. Initially, I was concerned about missing out on spontaneous collaboration, but I've found that planning questions for later has improved my productivity. While it’s not perfect—quick collaboration can be less convenient—finishing one demanding task without switching attention is incredibly satisfying. I wouldn’t want the focus blocks all day, but having them gives me the concentration and clear boundaries I need. I’ve learned to appreciate the value of focused work, even if it means less immediate interaction with colleagues.

#### B. B

Working in a role with personal scheduling constraints, fixed start times were a source of daily stress. With the introduction of flexible start and end times within agreed limits, I now have control over my schedule. This change requires clearer handovers with colleagues who have different hours, but it’s a small price to pay for the predictability and choice I now enjoy. I appreciate having control over my time more than working fewer hours. For me, the balance of predictability and choice is more important than having total freedom. This flexibility has allowed me to manage my personal commitments better, and I feel more in control of my day-to-day life.

#### C. C

When I was asked to mentor a newcomer, I initially saw it as extra work. I had no motivation at first and only agreed because the team needed help. However, explaining tasks to someone else has improved my understanding of my role. I also find satisfaction in seeing the newcomer become independent. While it takes patience and exposes habits I hadn’t questioned, the experience has made me value feeling useful to another person more than I expected. I started out reluctant, but now I’m quietly proud of the impact I’ve had. Mentoring has not only benefited the newcomer but has also enriched my own professional experience.

#### D. D

Our team was plagued by recurring operational problems, with people complaining separately without resolution. We started regular problem-solving meetings where everyone affected could contribute. Although these sessions can become repetitive if there’s no follow-up, they ensure shared responsibility and lead to action. Each meeting focuses on one issue, with a rotating person presenting it and the team deciding on the next step. I value this collaborative approach, but only when it results in concrete actions. Endless discussion without decisions is frustrating, but when it works, it’s very effective. The meetings have fostered a sense of teamwork and accountability, which I find very rewarding.

**49.** Who was concerned about missing out on spontaneous collaboration?

**50.** Who finds satisfaction in seeing others become independent?
